Project Proposal: A Scalable Ontology Framework for the Engine Digital Twin
1.0 Overview & Strategic Purpose

This project will deliver the intelligent data foundation for the Aircraft Engine Digital Twin. Our mission is to move beyond slow, siloed data analysis and create a unified, scalable framework that enables proactive and predictive maintenance. We will achieve this by developing a library of reusable Ontology Modeling Patterns and pioneering a Semantic ETL Framework to intelligently map our disparate data sources (HAMR, FST) into a central, meaningful model. This approach ensures our work is not a one-off solution, but a scalable blueprint for all future Digital Twin and enterprise analytics programs.
Phase 1: Foundational Patterns & Framework Prototype (Now – End of August)

Objective: To prove the value of our pattern-based approach by modeling the core of the engine lifecycle and demonstrating a significant new analytical capability. We will show that we can connect previously disconnected data to answer critical questions faster and more effectively than ever before.
Key Deliverables by End of August:

    A Library of Three Foundational Ontology Modeling Patterns:
    We will create and document generic, reusable models for:

        a. The Component Assembly Pattern: Defining how parts are related to each other and to parent assets (e.g., parenthood, installation, position).

        b. The Operational Event Pattern: Defining how real-world events (e.g., flights, sensor readings, fault codes) are linked to specific components.

        c. The Maintenance & Inspection Pattern: Defining how service actions (e.g., work orders, inspections, findings like coating loss) are recorded against a component.

    A Semantic ETL Framework (V1.0):
    We will develop and document a framework of tools and processes for mapping data to our ontology. This includes:

        Data Quality Profiling Tool: Scripts to automatically scan source tables for completeness, consistency, and validity against our target model.

        Data Transformation & Cleaning Engine: A set of reusable functions to normalize and cleanse raw source data (e.g., standardizing date formats, resolving conflicting codes).

        Semantic Mapping Tool: A documented process and set of mappings that connect columns from key HAMR and FST tables to the classes and properties in our ontology patterns.

    Live Knowledge Graph Demonstration:
    We will populate a pilot Knowledge Graph with integrated data and conduct a live demo that answers four foundational, high-value questions that are currently difficult to address:

        "What is the complete installation history of a specific part serial number across all engines?"

        "Show all recorded maintenance actions performed on that part."

        "Display all ESAS coating loss measurements for that part."

        "Correlate the part's flight hours with its inspection results on a single timeline."

Phase 2: Full Automation & Advanced Analytics (September – End of December)

Objective: To transition our successful prototype into a robust, automated, and high-performance Minimum Viable Product (MVP). We will expand our pattern library to cover the entire component lifecycle and demonstrate the system's ability to answer complex, predictive questions.
Key Deliverables by End of December:

    A Complete Library of Engine Lifecycle Modeling Patterns:
    We will expand our ontology to include patterns for Failure Analysis, Supply Chain & Logistics, and Financial Costing, creating a true 360-degree model.

    A Fully Automated Semantic ETL Framework:
    The V1.0 framework will be matured into a production-ready system within the enterprise Databricks environment. This includes:

        Automated Pipelines: All data transformations and mappings will be part of scheduled, monitored, and high-performance data pipelines.

        Automated Data Quality Alerts: The data quality profiler will be integrated into the pipelines to automatically flag and quarantine bad data, ensuring the Knowledge Graph remains trustworthy.

    Advanced DSQ Demonstration with a Rich Knowledge Graph:
    We will use the fully populated MVP Knowledge Graph to showcase its unique analytical power by answering advanced, system-level questions. The demo will focus on:

        The "Component Health Score": Demonstrating a predictive score for a part based on the synthesis of its entire operational and maintenance history.

        The "Geographical Wear & Tear" Analysis: Showing how the graph can effortlessly correlate deployment history at different bases with maintenance frequency and inspection results to identify "harsh" operating environments.
